# 2026-10-08 The priority fee cap is removed

The cap on the priority fee (5 gwei), which orders transactions within a block, is removed. The practice environment adopts it at its next restart, and the competition environment (every official regime) uses the same setting.

## Summary of changes

Two things change: how much you may bid, and how the environment updates prices. The ordering rule itself does not change.

### Items that change

- **The priority fee cap**: removed. A bid above 5 gwei is no longer refused by the RPC gateway and is no longer a violation in the post-run check.
- **How the reference price is updated**: not by transaction. The environment writes it directly into the contracts just before each block is made: the PriceFeed, Aave's oracles and GMX's oracle. No bid gets ahead of a price update.
- **Block production**: the environment makes each block after it has written the prices. The interval is 2 seconds, as before.
- **Fees on the environment's event trades**: the buys and sell-back of a launch wave, the sells and buy-back of a depeg, liquidity pulls and whale prints were fixed at the top of each block. From now on:
  - A launch wave's trades and a depeg's trades bid a fee proportional to what getting ahead of them is worth, drawn at random for each transaction.
  - Liquidity pulls and whale prints pay an ordinary fee.
  - The environment's trades carry a minimum output (a slippage limit). Moving the price past it cancels the environment's trade.

### Items that do not change

- **Ordering**: within a block, transactions execute highest priority fee first.
- **The rule that `maxFeePerGas` equals `maxPriorityFeePerGas`**: still required. A transaction whose `maxFeePerGas` is above its tip is refused by the gateway.
- **Price latency**: the reference price reaches observations one block late, as before.
- **Gas limit per transaction**: 10,000,000 gas.
- **The gas ETH endowment**: unchanged.

## What to do

If you use the reference runtime, your agent keeps trading, but **to bid above 5 gwei, update your checkout with `git pull`.** An old reference runtime with an old `config/practice.yaml` accepts bids only up to 5 gwei. The current runtime reads this setting from the manifest.

If you choose your own bids, also check the three points below.

1. **Review any bid that stops at 5 gwei.** With no cap, some participants will bid more than 5 gwei for contested opportunities. Equal bids are ordered by arrival.
2. **Fees come off your assets.** The priority fee you pay is taken from your ETH balance, and ETH is part of the asset value that is scored (rules §4.2). A bid above the opportunity's profit is a loss.
3. **`limits.maxPriorityFeePerGasWei` in the observation becomes 10^18 wei.** It is a nominal guard against a broken value, not a cap. The reference runtime accepts bids up to it. If you sign yourself, the gateway does not limit the amount.

## Background

With a cap, every participant going for a contested opportunity bids exactly the cap. Equal bids are not ordered by fee, so the winner was whoever arrived first. In the practice environment, several participants were sending at exactly the cap.

The cap existed to keep the environment's price update (6 gwei) at the top of each block. The prices are now written directly into the contracts, so the price update cannot be overtaken without a cap.

Every epoch of the competition starts everyone from the same endowment, so capital does not separate the bids either. What a bid is worth is set by the opportunity's profit, which is the same for everyone.
